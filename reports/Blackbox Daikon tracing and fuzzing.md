# Teach the tracer recursion, then give it input

The black-box front end already sees all the information it needs. What it lacks is a way to say it, and it cannot yet be fuzzed at all. On the 29-program Kvasir suite it scores **24% precision, 17% recall and 20% F1 (3,428 facts, 822 matched)**. Of the 3,975 oracle facts it still misses, **88.6% are type facts that are present in the execution but never emitted**. The single largest block is pointers inside structs two or more levels deep (2,142 facts, 53.9%) ([phase10.md](../docs/plans/phase10.md); [EVALUATION-KVASIR-SUITE.md](../docs/EVALUATION-KVASIR-SUITE.md)). The difficulty is that every recall mechanism tried so far lowered precision. `--deref-globals` raised recall from 17% to 20% and dropped precision to 16%, below the 20% floor. `--struct-globals` was a net loss of 123 matched facts. So the accuracy roadmap has two parts: first build ways to emit facts without inventing them (Kvasir-faithful A/V shadow memory, DynComp-style comparability, `hashcode` pointer roots, abstention), then build the recursive walker on top of them. The fuzzing harness is blocked on four specific things: there is no program input, every libc call except the allocator returns 0, unmapped memory silently becomes zero pages, and each iteration takes about 11 s, mostly process startup. The published precedents (InvsCov, Binvariants, unicornafl) give a clear design: a resident emulator with snapshot restore and a `place_input` callback, semantic stubs for input-consuming libc calls only, refutation by Daikon's own `InvariantChecker`, and replay-plus-oracle confirmation before an invariant is declared false. The roadmap at the end orders this work so that each step is measurable against the 20% floor before the next one starts.

## The tracer observes everything and says too little

The tracer runs a stripped, non-PIE x86-64 ELF under Unicorn starting at `main`. It derives arity, signedness, pointer kind, struct/array layout, heap bounds and globals from machine state alone, and DWARF is read only by the scorer ([TOOL.md](../docs/TOOL.md); [plans/README.md](../docs/plans/README.md)). Its **observation is essentially perfect**. All 198 of 198 program points have the same sample counts as Kvasir on real hardware. A soundness audit of 4,217 tier-8 facts found **0 false**, with 76.3% undecidable by the checker ([VERIFICATION.md](../docs/VERIFICATION.md)). The project's own words put it this way: "observation is perfect and its vocabulary is small" ([EVALUATION-KVASIR-SUITE.md](../docs/EVALUATION-KVASIR-SUITE.md)). On its own 10 fixtures the tracer scores 65/92/76 P/R/F1 ([EVALUATION.md](../docs/EVALUATION.md)). The suite numbers are the ones that matter.

Phase 10 traced the top four miss categories to **one structural bug: the dereference path is special-cased per root** (`_pointee_layout`, `_return_pointee_layout`, `_global_specs`) instead of recursing ([phase10.md](../docs/plans/phase10.md)). The tracer already knows which values are pointers (address-range classification against the live map) and what lies behind them (the global access map). It just never follows a pointer it loaded from a field. The breakdown of the 3,975 misses after tier 8 is:

| Missing fact | Count | Share | Current remedy and status |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pointer inside a struct, depth ≥2 | 2,142 | 53.9% | Mechanism A (recursive walker): not started |
| Global that is a pointer/array | 809 | 20.4% | B `--deref-globals`: landed, off (P 22→16%) |
| Global that is a struct | 355 | 8.9% | C `--struct-globals`: measured harmful (−123 matched) |
| Other / plain scalar | 254 | 6.4% | — |
| Local pointer/array → sequence | 215 | 5.4% | Covered by A |
| 8 bytes are a double | 110 | 2.8% | D runtime SSE tagging: not started |
| `char*` is a string | 90 | 2.3% | E `.rodata` strings: not started |

Sources: [phase10.md](../docs/plans/phase10.md). The depth distribution shows how far recursion has to go. Oracle facts sit **32.0% at depth 0, 30.8% at depth 1, 25.9% at depth 2**, so a depth limit of 2 covers 88% and a limit of 3 covers 93%. Cycles such as `n1->next->prev` make a visited-set mandatory ([phase10.md](../docs/plans/phase10.md)). That is close to Kvasir's own defaults, which the Daikon manual gives as `--nesting-depth=2` and `--struct-depth=4` ([Daikon manual](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/codespecs/daikon/master/doc/daikon.texinfo)). Phase 10 itself warns that A "should be expected to be worse, not better" for precision ([phase10.md](../docs/plans/phase10.md)). B's record supports the warning: it produced **6,111 facts to gain 176 matches**. If A is built without a precision mechanism underneath it, it will fall below the floor.

The failures so far share a cause: **evidence that is correct about the access but wrong about the object**. `--infer-from-caller` went from 239 to 375 facts on the fixtures for only 6 more matches, and precision fell from 65% to 43%. The cause was temporal aliasing: one stack address holds different objects over time, and the 64-byte window "invented three fields" on `identity_ptr` ([phase8.md](../docs/plans/phase8.md); [EVALUATION.md](../docs/EVALUATION.md)). `--struct-globals` failed because "a statically-initialised struct is never written at run time", so write-heterogeneity carries no information about loader-initialised data ([phase10.md](../docs/plans/phase10.md)). Both are provenance failures. That makes allocation-root provenance the precondition for A, not a refinement to add afterwards.

## Copying the oracle's own mechanisms is the safest route to precision

The most useful finding in the research comes from reading Fjalar's C source. **Kvasir gets its dynamic facts from Memcheck shadow bits, not from type inference.** Pointer validity uses A-bits (`addressIsAllocated`) and initializedness uses V-bits (`addressIsInitialized`). To bound a heap array, it probes forward while A-bits say "allocated", then walks back to the last element with any initialized byte, and returns `size-1`. It treats the current stack frame as allocated *and initialized* ("we are ASSUMING that it is!!!"). For pointers that do not land in a DWARF-known global array it "UNCONDITIONALLY RETURN[s] 0" ([fjalar_runtime.c](https://github.com/codespecs/fjalar/blob/master/valgrind/fjalar/fjalar_runtime.c)). Parameters at EXIT come from a "virtualStack" copy of the frame and its shadow state taken at entry ([fjalar_main.c](https://github.com/codespecs/fjalar/blob/master/valgrind/fjalar/fjalar_main.c)). Unicorn gives the tracer the whole address space, and it already has an allocator model, so per-byte A/V shadow state fed by the existing `UC_HOOK_MEM_WRITE` reproduces the oracle's semantics exactly. It also gives the "bound a stack pointee" remedy a principled form: an object is live from its first write in the current frame until frame exit. That remedy is worth 6 facts and today costs 136 false ones ([EVALUATION.md](../docs/EVALUATION.md)).

Two more oracle facts change the plan the project currently has. **First, modbits are not a lever for agreement with Kvasir.** EVALUATION.md calls hardcoded modbit 1 "the clearest remaining precision lever". But Kvasir's `mapInitToModbit` emits only 1 (initialized) or 2 (garbage), never 0 ([dtrace-output.c](https://github.com/codespecs/fjalar/blob/master/valgrind/fjalar/kvasir/dtrace-output.c)), and the developer manual says always emitting 1 "is safe" ([developer.texinfo](https://github.com/codespecs/daikon/blob/master/doc/developer.texinfo)). The tracer already matches the oracle here. If real modbits help at all, they help the fuzzing consumer, not the score, and Daikon can recompute them itself with `daikon.FileIO.add_changed=false` ([FileIO.java](https://github.com/codespecs/daikon/blob/master/java/daikon/FileIO.java)). **Second, comparability is a real gap, and it is verified locally.** `test.sh` runs `kvasir-dtrace` with no options and passes its separate `.decls` to Daikon ([test.sh](../test.sh), lines 200–224), so the oracle runs with DynComp on by default ([Daikon manual](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/codespecs/daikon/master/doc/daikon.texinfo)). The tracer, by contrast, assigns one comparability class per *kind* (scalar, pointer, float, global and so on) at tier 3 and above, and `var-comparability none` below tier 3 ([emulate.py](../scripts/tracer/emulate.py), lines 1450–1462 and 1872). Every scalar in a program point is therefore comparable to every other scalar. The manual names this as the main cause of "irrelevant properties… over unrelated variables" and says running without DynComp is "strongly discouraged" ([Daikon manual](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/codespecs/daikon/master/doc/daikon.texinfo)). DynComp's algorithm can be reproduced over Capstone-decoded instructions. Each value gets a tag, tags are copied on moves, loads and stores, operand tags are merged with union-find on arithmetic and compares (the `units` mode restricts this to add/sub/compare), and the union-find roots are numbered per program point, shared between ENTER and EXIT ([dyncomp_runtime.c](https://github.com/codespecs/fjalar/blob/master/valgrind/fjalar/kvasir/dyncomp_runtime.c); [ISSTA'06 abstract](https://homes.cs.washington.edu/~mernst/pubs/abstract-type-issta2006-abstract.html)). Nobody has measured how much DynComp improves precision on C or binary traces. The manual asserts it, and the original paper says it "significantly improved both the run time and precision of Daikon" ([CSAIL abstract](https://publications.csail.mit.edu/abstracts/abstracts06/jhp/jhp.html)).

Several cheaper precision tools exist in the trace format and Daikon's options. Declaring pointer-valued roots as `hashcode` makes Daikon treat them as "uninteresting for the purposes of output", which suppresses ordering and linear facts between addresses ([developer.texinfo](https://github.com/codespecs/daikon/blob/master/doc/developer.texinfo)). That directly targets B's explosion, where 65 of the 85 globals emitted are pointers printed as integers ([phase10.md](../docs/plans/phase10.md)). With `declared_type_comparability` on by default, distinct `dec-type` strings act as a second free partition ([VarInfo.java](https://github.com/codespecs/daikon/blob/master/java/daikon/VarInfo.java)). `min-value`/`max-value` attributes taken from load width suppress trivial bounds ([developer.texinfo](https://github.com/codespecs/daikon/blob/master/doc/developer.texinfo)). Raising `--conf_limit` only affects facts that held by chance, and Daikon "never outputs invariants that were ever false" ([Daikon manual](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/codespecs/daikon/master/doc/daikon.texinfo)). That makes it a lever for *claims handed to a fuzzer*, not for the Kvasir score, since the oracle runs at the default 0.99.

One gap stands apart from the rest because it **violates the rule outright instead of costing precision**. A by-value struct breaks arity: a 252-byte parameter is read as 18 arguments, and all 6 such cases on the suite are wrong. The docs call it "the one way to hand a consumer something actively wrong" ([EVALUATION-KVASIR-SUITE.md](../docs/EVALUATION-KVASIR-SUITE.md)). Under "invented facts are worse than absent", the minimum fix is to abstain: detect a run of stack-passed arguments that are copied as a block, and emit nothing rather than N scalars. Ranked by size, the smaller items come after the recursion work: strings E (90 facts, independent, "cheap, self-limiting"), runtime SSE tagging D ("well under 110"), and attributing an access to *every* matching pointer instead of the first, which is the memcpy two-buffer blind spot ([phase10.md](../docs/plans/phase10.md); [review-2026-09-17.md](../docs/plans/review-2026-09-17.md)). For strings and doubles, the dynamic type-recovery literature offers REWARDS-style "type sinks": a value is typed by the libc argument or SSE instruction it reaches, and otherwise left untyped ([REWARDS](https://friends.cs.purdue.edu/dokuwiki/doku.php?id=rewards)). That only works once libc calls are executed instead of skipped, which ties accuracy to the harness.

## Recursion should follow allocation roots, Howard-style, with abstention

The dynamic data-structure literature maps almost directly onto Mechanism A. Howard (NDSS'11) recovers structures from stripped gcc binaries in an emulator by tracking memory use ([Howard](https://www.syssec-project.eu/m/page-media/3/howard-ndss11.pdf)). It deliberately calls an array with fewer than 4 loop-accessed elements a struct, which trades false negatives for no false positives ([Howard](http://www.few.vu.nl/~herbertb/papers/howard_ndss11.pdf)). A third-party measurement puts Howard at **81.5% accuracy with only 59% function coverage** ([OSPREY S&P'21](https://www.youwei.site/papers/SP2021a.pdf)). Coverage is the limit on every dynamic method. MemPick classifies heap-graph shapes (lists, trees) only at "quiescent periods" ([MemPick](https://www.syssec-project.eu/m/page-media/3/mempick_wcre13.pdf)), and in a Daikon trace those periods are the ENTER and EXIT program points. The design that follows is to tag every pointer value with its root (malloc block, global extent, or frame), to recurse only through pointers whose root has a known extent (from the allocation size or from section-header and `.symtab` bounds), and to abstain when a root is unbounded or when one offset is accessed with incompatible kinds. That last condition is the union signature the type-recovery notes recommend flagging rather than resolving.

Static evidence has a monotone role in this design. The project's Stage-1 p-code checker already catches 86.2% of pointer-called-integer errors in Ghidra's parameter types with 0.7% false rejects, and reached 20/20 on hand check ([rea-type-checker.md](../docs/plans/rea-type-checker.md)). Dynamic evidence is an under-approximation, but every observed access is real, so it can only *add* refutations of a static guess ([dynamic_type_recovery notes](../research_notes/Blackbox%20Daikon%20tracing%20and%20fuzzing/dynamic_type_recovery.md)). The recent static systems are not a shortcut. TypeForge self-reports layout F1 of 88.2%, but an independent re-run by the Idioms authors got roughly 37–69 ([Awesome-REA issue](https://github.com/reacher-z/Awesome-REA/issues/592); [Idioms](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.04536)), and Ghidra and IDA recover almost no structs by default ([OSPREY](https://yonghwi-kwon.github.io/data/osprey_sp21.pdf)). None of this involves DWARF, so static priors are allowed at trace time, provided they are flag-gated and measured separately like any other heuristic.

## The harness needs input, honest libc, a crash policy and one resident process

Fuzzing is blocked at the first step. `_setup_memory` sets RDI=1 and points RSI and RDX into stack memory that is never written. Every non-allocator external call, and every indirect call, is skipped with RAX=0. No syscall hook exists. `_hook_mem_unmapped` maps a fresh zero page at any faulting address and carries on ([emulate.py](../scripts/tracer/emulate.py); [review-2026-09-17.md](../docs/plans/review-2026-09-17.md)). For a fuzzer each of these is a problem. RAX=0 from `strlen`, `strcmp`, `read` or `atoi` means "empty, equal, EOF, zero", so it **silently steers control flow down paths the real program would not take**. Zero-paging hides exactly the wild dereferences a fuzzer is supposed to find. The 17 suite programs left unmeasured because they need argv, stdin or files (gzip, bzip2, tcas, replace, print_tokens and others) are "the closest thing available to the fuzzing target" ([EVALUATION-KVASIR-SUITE.md](../docs/EVALUATION-KVASIR-SUITE.md)). Stage 3 of the type checker is blocked by the same problem: it "reached 6 program points in `cat`" ([rea-type-checker.md](../docs/plans/rea-type-checker.md)).

Bare Unicorn is "just a CPU emulator… not aware of… dynamic libraries, system calls, I/O handling". Qiling adds a loader, the real `ld.so`/glibc from a rootfs, and syscall emulation ([Qiling README](https://github.com/qilingframework/qiling)). A full move to Qiling would rebuild the tracer from scratch. The narrower option fits the project's rules better: keep Unicorn, and give real semantics, behind a flag, only to the input-consuming and string functions (`read`, `fgets`, `getc`, `fopen`/`fread`, `strlen`, `strcmp`, `memcpy`, `atoi`/`strtol`), with everything else still skipped. A stub that fabricates plausible memory is an invented fact. So each stub must either compute the real libc result from emulated memory, or mark its outputs `nonsensical`. Pointer-validity checks inside stubs should use the A/V shadow map from the accuracy track, so the two tracks share one mechanism.

Speed is a startup problem, not an emulation problem. Emulation is about **650 instructions, about 5 ms**. A tier-7 trace of test08 measured 0.40 s wall time, and a cold import of unicorn, capstone and elftools took 1.12 s. The rest of the documented ~11 s is presumably JVM startup and Daikon inference, but nobody has measured that split ([phase2.md](../docs/plans/phase2.md); [project_state_audit notes](../research_notes/Blackbox%20Daikon%20tracing%20and%20fuzzing/project_state_audit.md)). The standard fix is persistent execution. unicornafl's `uc_afl_fuzz` hands each input to a `place_input(uc, buf, len, iter, data)` callback in a loop that never re-forks ([unicornafl](https://github.com/Trolldemorted/unicornafl)). The AFL++ paper reports persistent mode at about 10× ([AFL++ WOOT'20](https://aflplus.plus/papers/aflpp-woot2020.pdf)), and Unicorn 2.1.4 optimised memory handling "for faster memory access and snapshots, especially useful for fuzzing" ([unicorn releases](https://github.com/unicorn-engine/unicorn/releases)). The JVM should leave the per-input loop entirely. Daikon has no incremental `.inv` update, but **`InvariantChecker` takes an `.inv` file plus new dtraces and reports every violating sample**, with `--conf` and `--filter` to restrict it to printed invariants. `MergeInvariants` "should be the same as running Daikon on the N original .dtrace files", and `--server` mode reads trace files as they appear ([Daikon manual](https://github.com/codespecs/daikon/blob/master/doc/daikon.texinfo)). The Python-side hot path, a `UC_HOOK_CODE` callback that disassembles every instruction again with Capstone `detail=True` (a 5× cost), justifies a two-tier design ([phase2.md](../docs/plans/phase2.md)). The fast path runs block coverage plus an in-process checker for the frozen invariants. The slow path runs the full trace and is taken only for inputs that add coverage or violate an invariant.

## Refutation must be confirmed, deduplicated and labelled by context

The two closest precedents both learn first and then freeze. InvsCov mines per-basic-block Daikon invariants on a seed corpus, compiles them in, and rewards violations as new state ([InvsCov README](https://github.com/eurecom-s3/invscov)). Binvariants does the same with register-level invariants on binaries, and states that it has no online re-learning and supports only single-block invariants ([Binvariants repo](https://github.com/FuturesLab/Binvariants)). It reports 143 bugs versus 137 for coverage-only fuzzing, and more than 27× unique violations; these are self-reported and seen only in snippets ([ACM DOI](https://doi.org/10.1145/3797078)). Neither uses violations as a bug oracle, and neither reports refuted invariants as false. That refute-and-report loop, over Daikon's procedure-level ENTER/EXIT grammar with pointer and struct derived variables, is where this project is new. "Binary-only invariant fuzzing" is already claimed by Binvariants. The literature also argues against human triage of the output: users misclassified **26–59% of incorrect invariants** in one study ([arXiv 1903.06089, citing Staats et al. ISSTA'12](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1903.06089)). SpecFuzzer is a direct precedent for using Daikon as a falsification filter over generated candidates ([SpecFuzzer](https://arxiv.org/abs/2201.10874v1)).

Three rules follow from the project's own principles. **A refutation is only a claim until it is confirmed.** With stubbed libc and synthesised argv, a violation can come from the model and not the program. A refuting input should be replayed on the slow path, then run under Kvasir via `test.sh` on the same input, and later natively, before the invariant is marked false. Kvasir's 198/198 sample-count agreement is already the project's fidelity yardstick for this ([VERIFICATION.md](../docs/VERIFICATION.md)). **Feedback must be deduplicated by invariant ID**, because a small seed corpus produces many cheap early refutations, each of which would otherwise count as coverage ([fuzzing_harness notes](../research_notes/Blackbox%20Daikon%20tracing%20and%20fuzzing/fuzzing_harness.md)). **Refutations from function-level fuzzing are "context-free"** and must be kept separate. Synthesised arguments can break preconditions that every real caller respects, so they refute facts that hold in every real execution. Re-inference is required as well as checking: `InvariantChecker` only tests invariants that already exist, and the weaker replacement (`i <= j` after `i < j` dies) only appears when Daikon is run again over the union, which `MergeInvariants` makes cheap to do in batches ([Daikon manual](https://github.com/codespecs/daikon/blob/master/doc/daikon.texinfo)). Address-valued facts need `--layouts > 1` throughout, because emulation is deterministic and a constant address would otherwise look like an invariant ([emulate.py](../scripts/tracer/emulate.py)).

## Prioritized roadmap

Each item is opt-in behind a flag or tier. It leaves tier 0 and the lower-tier goldens byte-identical, uses no DWARF outside `score_invariants.py`, and is reverted if it does not clear its pre-set criterion. The standing gates apply to every item: suite precision ≥20%, Daikon exits 0 on all 29 programs, own corpus ≥65/92/76, and `audit_soundness.py` reports 0 FALSE. Effort is this report's estimate for one engineer familiar with the code. It is not a project figure.

| # | Item | Targets (measured gap) | Expected impact | Effort | Falsifiable acceptance criterion |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Time the 11 s and make the tracer resident**: one ELF load, `context_save` plus a dirty-page snapshot at `main`, N inputs per process; batch Daikon over many traces | ~11 s/iter; ~15 min suite run | Unblocks every later item; one to two orders of magnitude per iteration (estimate) | S–M | Documented split of Python/JVM/Daikon time; resident mode is byte-identical to CLI output on all goldens; ≥20 traced inputs/s on test08 |
| 2 | **By-value struct abstention** (`--abstain-byval`) | 6/6 wrong arities, the only "actively wrong" output | Removes a whole class of false facts; small recall change | S | 0 of the 6 known cases emit N-scalar args; no fixture loses a matched fact; suite precision does not fall |
| 3 | **Pointer roots as `hashcode`, plus width-derived `min/max-value`**, applied to B | B at 16% precision, 65/85 globals are pointers printed as ints | Brings B's +176 matched within reach of the floor | S | B with item 3 reaches ≥20% suite precision while keeping ≥+150 matched over baseline; otherwise B stays off |
| 4 | **DynComp-lite comparability** (`--dyncomp`, `units` then `all`), per-ppt numbering shared ENTER/EXIT | Kind-class comparability vs the oracle's DynComp (verified in test.sh and emulate.py) | Unmeasured; the manual's main documented precision lever | M | Suite precision +≥3 points, matched facts fall ≤2%; result reported separately for `units` and `all` |

| 6 | **Recursive walker A**, root-provenance gated, depth 1 then 2, visited-set, abstain on unbounded root or conflicting kinds | 2,142 + 215 facts (59.3% of misses); estimates of ~2,400–3,200 are inconsistent | Largest recall lever; precision risk flagged by Phase 10 | L | NestedStructTest >150 matched (pre-set, from 10); suite precision ≥20% at depth 1 before depth 2 is attempted; standing gates hold |

| 10 | **Coverage-guided fast path** (unicornafl), violation map deduplicated by invariant ID, slow-path tracing only on new coverage or violations | Python per-instruction hook cost | Fuzzing at useful throughput | M | ≥100 exec/s fast path on test08-class targets (the notes' estimate, not a measurement); fewer than 1 slow-path trace per 50 inputs once coverage saturates |

| 12 | **Distance-to-violation feedback** (IJON/FuzzFactory style) and a native QEMU-user differential check | Slow refutation of `x <= c` / `x == y` facts; emulator-model fidelity | Faster refutation; catches stub infidelity | M | Time-to-first-refutation falls ≥2× on fixtures compared with item 10 alone; 0 unexplained emulated/native output divergences on the 17 programs |

Item 1 comes first because Phase 10 already notes that a 15-minute suite run limits how many experiments can be measured, and every later item has to be measured. Items 2–5 come before 6 because they are the precision budget that A will spend. Item 8 can run in parallel with 4–6, since it touches only the call and unmapped-access paths. Deliberately left off the list: real modbits (the oracle never emits 0, so they cannot improve agreement; test `add_changed=false` only for the fuzzing consumer); defaulting `--struct-globals` (measured harmful and rooted in write evidence that does not exist for statically initialised data); and a full Qiling migration (it rebuilds the tracer for fidelity that item 8 delivers selectively).

### What is verified and what is not

**Verified against local code or primary source:**

- The miss table, precision history, failure analyses, harness blockers and timing figures come from the project's own docs and code at HEAD 2137177.
- That `test.sh` runs Kvasir with default options (DynComp on, separate `.decls`), and that the tracer uses kind-class comparability, were checked in [test.sh](../test.sh) and [emulate.py](../scripts/tracer/emulate.py) for this report.
- The Fjalar A/V-bit, heap-probe, frame-snapshot and modbit behaviour were read from Fjalar's C source.
- Daikon option semantics (`conf_limit`, `hashcode`, `InvariantChecker`, `MergeInvariants`, `add_changed`, `is_param` filtering) were read from the Daikon texinfo and Java source at commit e96cd15. That is the 5.9.1 text and was not diffed against the project's 5.8.x.

**Unverified or snippet-level:**

- **Literature figures:** the Binvariants and InvsCov numbers, Howard's 81.5%, and the TypeForge and Idioms F1 figures come from search summaries, not full papers.
- **Throughput:** the persistent-mode speedups, unicornafl throughput, and the 100 exec/s and 20 inputs/s targets are estimates.
- **Fidelity and attribution:** Qiling's glibc compatibility is unchecked. The attribution of the ~11 s to the JVM is inferred from a 0.40 s tracer measurement and was not measured.
- **Mechanisms:** the hypothesised mechanism of the `is_param` coefficient defect is untested.
- **Unmeasured effects:** the precision gain from DynComp on binary traces, the yield of Mechanism A, and how `InvariantChecker` behaves at fuzzing scale have not been measured by anyone.

## Conclusion

Measured as an inference problem, the tracer is close to its ceiling. Phase 10 puts the information-theoretic residue at about 5% of the oracle ([phase10.md](../docs/plans/phase10.md)). As an expression problem, roughly 80% of the oracle is still unsaid. The past failures show that the limit on saying more is provenance: whether the tracer knows *which object* an access belongs to. That makes the shadow-memory and comparability items more than polish: they are what makes recursion affordable under a 20% floor. The research also changes one assumption in the current plan: modbits do not cap agreement with Kvasir, but comparability may.

On the fuzzing side, this project would be new for how it handles results, not for its feedback channel. Binvariants already shows that binary-only invariant feedback finds bugs. No published system confirms refutations against an independent oracle and reports the refuted invariants as false. A harness that replays each refutation and checks it with Kvasir before calling an invariant false would be the strict, rule-consistent version of what Binvariants and InvsCov do. It would also yield a precision metric based on refutation by some execution, which does not depend on the oracle's own coverage limits.
